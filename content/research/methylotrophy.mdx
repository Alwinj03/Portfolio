## Can you teach *E. coli* to eat methanol and make amino acids?

That's the question behind my research, presented at the **International Conference on Bioengineering** (SRMIST, Kattankulathur).

I built a genome-scale metabolic model of *E. coli* in **COBRApy** to simulate synthetic methylotrophy for sustainable L-lysine production.

What I did:

- Reconstructed a genome-scale metabolic model (GEM) of *E. coli*
- Dropped in the RuMP pathway enzymes (MDH, HPS, PHI)
- Added a redox balancing strategy
- Redirected carbon flux toward the Aspartate–DAP lysine pathway
- Ran Flux Balance Analysis (FBA) to predict growth + theoretical lysine yield

**Why methanol?** Normal amino acid production leans on sugar fermentation — which chains industrial biotech to agriculture. Methanol can be made from captured CO₂, making it a clean **C1 feedstock** for circular, low-carbon biomanufacturing.
