## What if we could read the cow's gut like a network, not a black box?

A cow's rumen is one of nature's best biomass reactors, a dense microbial community that tears down tough lignocellulosic plant matter into usable energy. **CMIN** reconstructs that community *computationally*: which microbes are there, what each one metabolizes, and how they feed each other. The endgame is understanding rumen microbiota well enough to inform **biofuel and sustainable bioprocessing** research.

I worked on this during my **INSA–IASC–NASI Summer Research Fellowship** at **IIT Kharagpur**, under **Dr. Amit Ghosh** — replicating and extending the metabolic interaction network methodology of **Mondal et al. (2022)**.

### What I built

I was assigned **35 microbial species** to characterize from the ground up, then wire into two networks:

- a **Community-wide Metabolic Interaction Network (CMIN)**, the full web of who exchanges what
- a **species-level metabolic interaction network**, the same logic zoomed into individual bacteria

### The pipeline

For uncharacterized organisms, I ran a full genome-annotation stack:

- **RAST** for genome annotation
- **dbCAN2 / dbCAN3** for CAZyme profiling (the carbohydrate-active enzymes that do the actual biomass breakdown)
- **BlastKOALA** for KEGG pathway + KO-number confirmation of metabolic outputs

Every predicted metabolite had to clear a **KO-number threshold**, if the genome didn't support it, it got cut, even when it was biologically plausible.

### What the networks revealed

The interesting part is the **cross-feeding**: microbes quietly handing each other the compounds they can't make themselves. A few functional signatures I traced:

- **B12 / cobalamin donors** feeding vitamin-dependent neighbors
- **butyrate producers**, distinguished by *mechanism* (Etf complex vs. butyrate kinase)
- **xylanolytic / pectinolytic degraders** breaking down the hard plant polymers

I structured all of it to match the Mondal et al. supplementary-table formats, then built the final **CMIN and species-level networks in Cytoscape** from Python-generated edge lists and cross-feeding predictions.

### Why it matters

Map the rumen as an engineered system and you can start asking real questions — which microbes to enrich, which metabolic handoffs drive biomass breakdown, and how to point all of it toward **low-carbon biomanufacturing and biofuels**.
